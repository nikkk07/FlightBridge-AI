Approved Training Organisations (ATOs), Language Testing Bodies (LTBs)
and Pilot Training Organisations (PTOs) Conducting:
    •   Courses of Flight Training
    •   Courses of Theoretical Knowledge Training
    •   Assessments of Aviation English Language Proficiency


The Civil Aviation Authority approves courses of flying training, theoretical knowledge
training and assessments of English language proficiency to be conducted by
Approved Training Organisations, Language Testing Bodies and Pilot Training
Organisations as listed in this document.
• To gain approval for the conduct of a training course for a UK Part-FCL Licence,
   Rating or Certificate, such as an Aeroplane Instrument Rating (Restricted), the
   training organisation must comply with the applicable requirements of the UK
   Aircrew Regulation, reference UK Reg 1178/2011 (as amended).
• To gain approval for the conduct of a training course for a UK National Pilot
   Licence, Rating or Certificate, such as a Helicopter Rotorway Executive Type
   Rating or an Aeroplane IMC Rating, the training organisation must comply with
   the applicable requirements of The Air Navigation Order 2016 (as amended).
• In general, UK National requirements will be equivalent to those laid down in UK
   Part-FCL. This is to ensure similar licensing and oversight standards and to
   simplify compliance for organisations seeking to hold approvals that comply with
   both UK National and UK Part-FCL regulations.

NOTES:

1) Medical
     The CAA strongly recommends that prospective students ensure before
     committing to any training or associated costs they meet the applicable medical
     requirements and will be able to obtain an appropriate medical certificate for the
     issue or re-issue of a licence, certificate, rating or any other required qualification.

     The UK CAA can only issue a UK Part-FCL licence or a UK National licence to
     an applicant for whom it holds appropriate medical and licensing records. An
     applicant whose medical and licensing records are held by a non-UK aviation
     authority must apply to that authority for evidence of licensing records and
     medicals to be provided to the UK CAA.

     The information in this document is produced for general information and

     regulation, CAP or other document issued by the CAA.
